Problem Statement

Manual or phone-based food ordering can cause wrong orders, delayed deliveries, and poor order tracking. The proposed Food Delivery System lets customers browse restaurants, place orders, pay online and track delivery. Restaurants manage menus and orders, and delivery agents handle dispatch through a centralized system.

Goal: Apply Object-Oriented Design (OOD) to create a modular, reliable, and maintainable food ordering application.

System Objectives

  • Search restaurants by cuisine, rating, and location
  • View menus and prices before ordering
  • Place, update, and cancel orders
  • Maintain customer and order information
  • Process payments and confirm orders
  • Allow restaurants to manage menu items

Real-World Scenario

How apps like Zomato, Swiggy or Uber Eats work, and where object-oriented design fits in.

1. Customer orders

Priya opens the app on a busy evening, searches for "paneer tikka" near her home, compares ratings and delivery time, and adds items to her cart.

👨‍🍳

2. Restaurant prepares

The restaurant receives the order on its dashboard, accepts it, and marks the items as being prepared. Sold-out dishes are switched off from the menu.

🛵

3. Agent delivers

The nearest delivery agent is assigned, picks up the food and updates the status step by step until it is delivered to the doorstep.

Why a manual process fails

Wrong or missed orders on phone calls
No live tracking or delivery estimate
Cash-only payments and no records

How this design solves it

Every order is a tracked Order object with a status
Payment class handles UPI, card and cash uniformly
Clear classes make the app easy to extend and test

Object-Oriented Design

The core principles and main classes used in the system.

Core OOD Principles

Encapsulation

Keep data and related operations within classes.

Abstraction

Hide internal complexity behind simple operations.

Inheritance

Customer, Restaurant and DeliveryAgent share common properties through a User class.

Polymorphism

Different payment methods (UPI, card, cash) implement the same payment operation.

System Architecture

Presentation LayerInterface for customers, restaurants, delivery agents and administrators.
⇅
Business Logic LayerMenu search, order processing, payments and delivery assignment.
⇅
Data LayerStores customer, restaurant, menu, order and payment information.

Purpose: Separating these layers improves organization, testing, and maintainability.

Quality, Impact & Limitations

Quality Assurance

  • Functional: searching, ordering, payment, cancellation
  • Availability: block out-of-stock items
  • Validation: customer details and address
  • Security: protect data, role-based access
  • Integration: order, payment and delivery modules

Benefits & Future Scope

Benefits

  • Faster ordering and restaurant management
  • Fewer manual errors and wrong orders
  • Easier maintenance, testing and extension

Future enhancements

  • AI-based food recommendations
  • Cloud-based data management
  • Mobile apps and live location tracking
  • Secure online payment integration

Limitations & Challenges

  • Handling heavy order traffic during peak hours
  • Protecting customer information and payment details
  • Maintaining accurate menu availability and delivery times
